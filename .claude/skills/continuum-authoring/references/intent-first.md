# Continuum authoring: intent-first authoring

Part of the `continuum-authoring` skill ([index](../SKILL.md)). Loaded when the work needs it.

## 5. Intent-first authoring (the default)

Every session is intent-first unless the human explicitly asks for development-ready output. Completeness means the consequential intent is preserved, not that every artifact exists.

**Choosing the mode.** *Development-ready mode* (`development-ready.md` §8) applies only on an explicit request from the human: a development package, development-ready or implementation-ready output, a handoff package for a coding agent. It is then never silently downgraded: every `development-ready.md` §8 row applies, and what cannot be met makes the result Partial with the missing rows named. Everything else is intent-first, and is never silently upgraded into package production. If the request is unclear, work intent-first and say in one line that development-ready mode is available.

**The conversation.** Discover → Decide → Preserve → Connect to evidence → Evolve. A loop you drive in conversation, not phases to complete.

1. **Understand** the product or the proposed change: the intended outcome, its boundaries, and what would be costly to reverse. Read the repository, any existing `continuum/` model, its lifecycle state and whether an adopted package exists (`package-and-lifecycle.md` §4), and the decision space first; do not ask what they already answer. Note the session's start time. **Code but no model**: do not recover the whole system; model only the existing elements the decisions touch, cite each (`path:start-end`), and record the unmodelled remainder as out of scope.
2. **Discover the consequential decisions and constraints.** A decision is consequential when it is costly to reverse, crosses a boundary (ownership, trust, data, consistency, an external dependency), or could be silently erased by a later change. Name the few that matter now, and say what does not need deciding or modelling yet. **Check the guarantee before relying on it.** When a decision depends on transactions, event delivery or ordering, idempotency, concurrency or recovery after a crash or restart, read the code that would provide it (transaction boundaries, where an event is recorded and dispatched, how a delivery is claimed, what runs at startup, locks or conditional writes) and cite it, before recording the decision. Walk the failure windows explicitly: a crash between a commit and a publish, between claiming and handling a delivery, during a restart, and two writers racing. A model declaration such as `delivery[at-least-once]` is a claim, not evidence that the implementation delivers it. A guarantee the code does not provide is a gap: one the proposal introduces is fixed in the proposal; a pre-existing one is recorded with its evidence and the smallest fix, not repaired as part of the proposal (`package-and-lifecycle.md` §4); and the verification classification names the crash or race test that would show it.
3. **Ask only questions that change the intent**, batched, each with why it matters and a recommended default. Decide minor ones yourself and record them. **Challenge, do not transcribe:** when a stated choice looks questionable (needless distribution, shared write ownership, an irreversible choice made early, a constraint another one contradicts), say so with the reason and a simpler alternative. The human decides; record the agreement (`authority-and-tools.md` §1 authority 2), or the disagreement and its risk. **Challenge before recording:** raise a consequential challenge before the decision is written down, with the question when you see it then, or, when an answer introduces a choice you would challenge, in one short follow-up before preserving it. Only a risk you could not have seen before writing is recorded afterwards, marked as found after the decision.
4. **Preserve the intent in the artifact that carries it**, and only there (how to author each: `artifacts.md` §6):

   | Intent | Preserved in |
   |---|---|
   | structure, ownership, boundaries, messages, workflows, dependencies, Environments | ADL (new model, or an `.adl-change` on an existing one), compiled (`authority-and-tools.md` §2) |
   | business rules, lifecycles, obligations, actors | BDL, only when business semantics are the hard part |
   | a consequential technology choice | a decision (row below) until the model declares the Environment it belongs to; then a binding entry for the affected element, recorded as proposed until agreed |
   | a decision, its alternatives and rationale | the Host decision space (`continuum_decision_propose`) when there is one; otherwise the *Decisions* section of the intent record (`package-and-lifecycle.md` §4). Proposed until the human agrees (`authority-and-tools.md` §1) |
   | a cross-cutting rule (e.g. tenant isolation) | a principle: check existing ones (`continuum_principle_list`, `continuum_principle_check`); propose a new one as a decision, never accept it; where ADL can declare it as a requirement or constraint, do so |
   | a picture that settles a question or communicates the design | an exploratory diagram (`diagrams.md` §7), labelled as its `delivery.kind` says |
   | the intent itself, assumptions with provenance, open questions | the intent record: `GOAL.md`, or with an adopted package the proposal record (`package-and-lifecycle.md` §4) |

   A thin compiling model that holds the agreed decisions is enough. Do not author elements nobody decided, or bind Environments nobody chose.
5. **Connect to evidence.** Classify each consequential expectation as **verifiable** (a declared Continuum check, acceptance scenario or test could evaluate it: name which), **indirectly testable** (only by proxy: inspection, load test, review), or **human judgment** (fitness, trade-off and risk acceptance). Recommend verification only for the few high-consequence, testable invariants (for example mutation authority or tenant isolation), never a verifier per rule. Report evidence that exists with the tool that produced it; everything else is unverified (`authority-and-tools.md` §1).
6. **Stop at sufficient clarity**: every consequential decision is agreed with its rationale preserved, or explicitly open with an owner and what it blocks; touched ADL and BDL compile; the evidence classification is written; and implementation can proceed on what is decided. Then write the session outcome and stop. Offer development-ready mode; do not start it.
7. **Evolve.** On a later change, find the intent it affects, update only those artifacts (an existing model changes through an `.adl-change`), and surface conflicts with earlier intent instead of silently rewriting it. If an adopted package exists, the change is a proposal (`package-and-lifecycle.md` §4) and the proposal record says which readiness claims would have to be re-established for the proposed state. With no adopted package but an earlier development-ready package, say that its readiness claim no longer holds for the changed state, and keep its handoff as `HANDOFF-<date>.md` rather than overwriting it.

**Switching modes.** A development-ready request mid-conversation starts `development-ready.md` §8 from the current state: reuse agreed intent and run `development-ready.md` §8's discovery for whatever it lacks. On an adopted package it prepares the proposed change for the lifecycle; it never adopts it. A human may also end a development-ready session early; that is an explicit agreement (`authority-and-tools.md` §1 authority 2), and the result is Partial with the missing rows named.

**Not part of the default.** No binding of every Environment, no acceptance suite, no final verified diagram set, no package build, no review. Each is used when the request, the risk or the evidence warrants it. One fresh review (`development-ready.md` §8) is worth its cost when a costly-to-reverse decision rests on your reasoning alone, or the human asks; otherwise say SELF-REVIEW.

**Bound the investment.** Track cumulative effort: elapsed time, tool calls, subagent invocations, diagram runs, compile rounds. When effort grows without adding preserved intent, stop and say so.

**Session outcome** (`HANDOFF.md`, or with an adopted package the proposal record, `package-and-lifecycle.md` §4). One short record; no section repeats another:
- a status line (on an adopted package, the `package-and-lifecycle.md` §4 opening statement) and the intent in two or three sentences;
- *Decisions*: one table row each: decision, rationale, agreement (who and date, `authority-and-tools.md` §1 authority 2) or open, and where it is preserved (element or file);
- *Open questions*: question, recommended default, owner, what it blocks;
- *Verification*: expectation, class (step 5), how it would be checked or what evidence exists;
- proposed material not in any artifact (binding entries, scenario sketches, gaps), only when there is some;
- *Evidence and effort*: verdicts of tools that ran, what was not checked, review or SELF-REVIEW, elapsed time and tool calls, and the next steps in one line.

Do not restate the decisions in prose or close with a summary of the record. With no adopted package, `GOAL.md` holds the intent, *Decisions*, *Open questions* and *Verification*, and `HANDOFF.md` only the status line, a pointer to `GOAL.md`, any proposed material and *Evidence and effort*. End `GOAL.md` with: "The Goal explains intent. The semantic ADL defines architectural meaning. If they conflict, the semantic model governs."
